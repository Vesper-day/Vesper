# Layer 1: Foundation

## Layer Purpose

Layer 1 locks the foundational decisions that every subsequent decision depends on. These are the non-negotiables that, if changed later, would invalidate work done in subsequent layers. The goal of this layer is to make every later decision easier by clarifying who the product is for, what it stands for, what it feels like, and what success looks like.

## Target User

**Primary audience: Young professional.** Ages 22 to 32. In the first decade of a real career. Has disposable income. Actively building "adult routines" such as consistent fitness, real meals, sleep hygiene, and financial habits on top of a demanding job. Lives on mobile but does deeper configuration on desktop. High willingness to pay for tools that deliver measurable time or cognitive savings.

**Secondary audience: Ambitious student.** College or graduate level, juggling classes, side projects, social life, and aspirational health routines. Tight budget. Mobile-first behavior. Important as a future-young-professional pipeline and as a present-day early-adopter base.

**Power-user layer: Quantified-self optimizer.** Tracks data via wearables, monitors macros and sleep, wants depth and visualizations. This is a paid-tier feature set rather than a separate target audience. It justifies a premium pricing tier without diluting the core product positioning.

**Explicitly not targeted at launch:** Overwhelmed multitaskers and parents (concentrated willingness to pay but distinct workflows that would dilute focus). ADHD-first or executive-function users (already well served by Tiimo and similar tools). Fitness-first users (fitness is one feature among many in this product, not the core hook).

## One-Line Pitch

"It's the app that schedules your whole day for young professionals."

## Positioning

**Primary lens, dominant in marketing and design:** Life OS with local intelligence. Two combined ideas: one unified app for every domain of daily life (work, food, fitness, errands), and deep awareness of the user's physical environment such as nearby grocery stores, gyms, restaurants, and traffic patterns.

**Secondary lens, background support:** Adaptive planning. The user does a quick mood and energy check-in and the plan reshapes accordingly. This is not the headline feature but reinforces the warm, responsive, anti-rigid feel.

**Paid-tier lens:** Quantified life. Trend charts, weekly reviews, correlation insights. This becomes a major selling point for the premium plan and serves the optimizer power-user.

## Voice and Tone

The app's voice is modeled on Jarvis from Iron Man, framed as a butler rather than a chatbot. The tone is warm but professional. Calm rather than enthusiastic. Quietly competent. The voice delivers outputs without seeking validation, does not use emojis or exclamation points, and never refers to itself as an AI. The brand's external marketing voice matches this in-app voice for consistency.

## Brand Mood and Aesthetic Direction

The brand's visual and emotional mood is **warm darkness**, not cold darkness. The reference images live in dimly-lit libraries at dusk, hotel concierge desks after hours, and old manor studies that house modern function inside their walnut paneling. The product feels like a refined estate where someone competent is quietly handling things on your behalf.

This direction is set here in Layer 1 because it is foundational: it informs the name selection, the design language, the marketing aesthetic, the tone of copy, and even the texture of microinteractions in the app. The full visual system (specific color tokens, typography selections, spacing scales) is finalized in Layer 4. What follows in this section is the strategic mood direction that Layer 4 must execute against.

**Mood words:** dusk, amber, library, velvet, manor, twilight, hearth, concierge, study, refinement, ember, quiet competence.

**Color palette concept (preview, locked in Layer 4):**

- Primary background: deep charcoal or rich espresso. Not pure black. Not stark.
- Text: cream or aged paper white. Not stark white.
- Primary accent: bronze, amber, or warm gold. The "lamplight" color.
- Secondary accent (use sparingly): deep forest green or oxblood burgundy.
- Strictly avoided: stark white, neon blue, pastel anything, bright saturated accents, beige farmhouse warmth.

**Differentiation from competitors:**

- Motion and Linear use cold dark (blue-black, slate). The product instead uses warm dark (espresso, charcoal with amber).
- Reclaim uses bright calendar blue. The product avoids cool palettes entirely.
- Tiimo uses playful saturated colors. The product is the opposite emotional register: still and refined rather than energetic.
- Headspace, Calm, and lifestyle wellness apps use soft beige and pastel warmth. The product avoids that "lifestyle brand" softness; the warmth here is sophisticated rather than cozy.

**Why this mood specifically:**

- It reinforces the butler tone (a butler operates in a refined space, not a bright modern office).
- It supports anti-overwhelm positioning (warm dark is restful; bright is stimulating).
- It creates immediate premium feel without needing to declare premium.
- It is genuinely distinct from every named competitor in the productivity space.
- The design carries the modernity while the brand carries the warmth, which neutralizes any "lifestyle brand" risk that warm-themed names might otherwise introduce.

## Geographic Scope

V1 launches in the United States only. The features that depend on local context (grocery stores, restaurants, transit) are best calibrated to a single country at first. English-speaking expansion to Canada, the United Kingdom, and Australia comes once US retention is proven. Non-English translations are explicitly post-acquisition or post-major-funding territory and are not part of the early product roadmap.

## Platform Strategy

Both web and mobile ship on day one. They serve distinct purposes within a single product.

**Mobile app:** the daily revenue surface. Users open it throughout the day to see what comes next, mark items complete, do energy check-ins, and interact with the active plan. Mobile supports real editing including drag-and-reorder of blocks, full block detail views, and natural-language input, but remains optimized for execution rather than configuration. Deeper planning workstation features live on web.

**Web app:** the planning workstation. Deeper configuration, full schedule editing, integrations management, account settings, and the quantified-life dashboards all live here. Users will typically use the web app weekly for planning sessions and the mobile app daily for execution.

## Success Criteria

Three months after public launch:

- 5,000 total user signups, free and paid combined
- 100 paying subscribers

These targets function as decision triggers rather than ceilings. Hitting them on schedule triggers investment in scaling infrastructure and possibly paid acquisition. Missing them by half triggers a product-market-fit diagnostic. Beating them by five times triggers a re-evaluation of the indie versus startup path.

## AI Disclosure Philosophy

The product uses AI as a core engine but markets itself as an assistant, not as an AI tool. The brand never leads with "AI-powered" language in marketing copy, app store listings, or social presence. Disclosure happens where it is legally required (terms of service, privacy policy) and where users actively ask. This is a deliberate response to the growing consumer stigma against AI products and aligns with the butler framing: a competent assistant whose mechanism is invisible to the user.

## Build-in-Public Strategy

Hybrid stealth. The founder does not appear on camera, and the founder's identity is not disclosed. An anonymous brand account is established on X and TikTok early in the build process and posts product previews, screen recordings, philosophy content, and warm-aesthetic visuals. The marketing voice on these accounts matches the in-app butler tone exactly.

The first three to four months of the build are quiet, with low-volume posts. Posting volume ramps up around month five as launch approaches. Launch itself is loud: Product Hunt feature, full waitlist email blast, and coordinated social posts across all channels.

Layer 6 specifies a high-volume content strategy generated primarily by Claude with founder review. The prior weekly time cap of three to five hours has been lifted in favor of a Claude-driven generation pipeline. Founder content time is approximately 30 minutes per week of Sunday batch review. Content volume targets are 7-10 X posts per week, 3-4 Instagram posts per week, 2-3 TikTok videos per week, 1-2 Reddit comments per week during the build phase, and 1 long-form essay per month on the Vesper blog.

## Founder Identity Strategy

The founder remains anonymous until a major relevance milestone is reached. The exact threshold is intentionally left flexible in this layer and will be revisited as the project progresses. Candidate thresholds include reaching $50,000 monthly recurring revenue, securing a major press feature, entering an acquisition conversation, or the founder simply reaching personal comfort with public attention.

## Name

**Status: deferred.** The name is not locked in Layer 1 and will be revisited closer to Layer 4 (Experience and Identity) or before public launch. Several premium candidates have been explored and rejected for .com availability or trademark conflicts. The current direction favors dignified, multi-syllable, classically-rooted names (Latin, Greek, or refined English) over short single-syllable invented words or compound smashed-together brand names.

**Working assumption for documents and code:** the product can be referred to by any placeholder name during the build. Final name selection blocks marketing materials and app store listings but does not block build progress.

**Direction for final selection when revisited:**

- Premium, dignified, multi-syllable
- Latin, Greek, or classical etymology preferred
- No compound words ("Daysmith," "Hourkeep" style rejected)
- No flat single-syllable invented words
- Aesthetically aligned with warm-dark dusk-manor brand mood
- Acceptable to use a thematically-appropriate alternative TLD (.life, .house, .care, .haus, .studio) rather than force a compromised .com
- Strictly avoid .net, .mobi, and other TLDs that downgrade premium perception
- Willing to pay reasonable premium for the right domain

**Names explored and rejected:** Archaia (.com taken by comic publisher, meaning misaligned). Numa, Hearth, Aspen, Wexley, Veyla, Hovi, Brevin (all rejected for availability conflicts or insufficient brand fit). Halcyon was the strongest match conceptually (the meaning "calm period" aligns directly with anti-overwhelm positioning) but .com was unavailable and .app was priced at $15,000. Verity, Linea, and Selene were strong dignified contenders in the same register.

## Ambition Track

The default trajectory is indie scale with acquisition as the eventual exit. The targeted state after the first year is $5,000 to $20,000 in monthly recurring revenue with the founder remaining solo. Acquisition conversations become plausible once the product reaches $100,000 to $500,000 in annual recurring revenue and demonstrates strong retention. The startup path (raising venture capital, hiring a team, scaling aggressively) is held in reserve and only pursued if growth becomes explosive enough that capital constraints would limit otherwise inevitable success.

## What Was Considered and Rejected

The following decisions were actively considered during Layer 1 and rejected, recorded here so they are not revisited later:

- **ADHD-first positioning:** rejected because Tiimo and similar tools already own that audience well.
- **Multitasker and parent positioning:** rejected because the workflows and pricing psychology differ enough from young professionals to dilute focus.
- **Fitness-first positioning:** rejected because fitness is one of many features and a fitness-first identity would mislead users about the breadth of the product.
- **"Everyone who wants to be more productive":** rejected as a target audience because it provides no design constraints and no marketing focus.
- **Loud, founder-visible build-in-public:** rejected due to founder's preference for anonymity and the AI-stigma constraint that limits how loudly the product can claim its mechanism.
- **Startup track from day one:** rejected because the project economics work as a solo indie and venture funding would commit the founder to growth expectations that may not match personal goals.
- **Cold dark aesthetic (Motion/Linear style):** rejected because it conflicts with the butler/warmth positioning. Warm dark is the chosen direction instead.
- **Bright/light/playful aesthetic:** rejected because it conflicts with anti-overwhelm and dignified positioning.
- **Compound smashed-together names:** rejected as a naming pattern because they read as effortful and non-premium.
- **Short single-syllable invented names:** rejected as a naming pattern because they lack the dignity and weight the brand mood requires.
- **Cheap-perception TLDs (.net, .mobi, .info):** rejected because they undermine the premium positioning.
